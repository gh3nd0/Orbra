Mid-contract price rises on broadband and mobile

Last updated: [fill in when checked]

Quick answer

For phone, broadband and pay-TV contracts taken out since 17 January 2025, any planned price rise must be shown in pounds and pence before you sign up, along with when it happens. Rises linked to inflation or set as a percentage aren’t allowed in new contracts.

The rule since January 2025

Ofcom banned inflation-linked and percentage-based price rises in new phone, broadband and pay-TV contracts. Instead, providers must:

  • show any price rise in pounds and pence (for example, “£2 a month more from April”)
  • tell you when each rise will happen
  • make this clear before you sign up

The rule doesn’t cap prices. It means you know exactly what you’ll pay from the start.

Older contracts

If you signed up before 17 January 2025, your contract may still have rises linked to inflation (such as CPI plus a percentage) or set as a percentage.

Check your own contract

  1. Find your contract summary or welcome email.
  2. Look for the price-rise terms: the amount and the month it applies.
  3. Add the date to your calendar, or to Orbra, so the new price doesn’t surprise you.

If your provider raises your price by more than your contract says, you may be able to leave without a fee. See leaving a contract after a price rise.

Questions

Can my broadband provider still raise prices mid-contract?

Yes, but in contracts taken out since 17 January 2025, any rise must be shown in pounds and pence before you sign up, with the date it happens.

Does the rule apply to my old contract?

Not if you signed up before 17 January 2025. Older contracts may still include rises linked to inflation or set as a percentage.

What if my provider didn’t tell me the rise in pounds and pence?

Ofcom says to complain to your provider if it didn’t show the price rise in pounds and pence, or didn’t say when it would apply.
