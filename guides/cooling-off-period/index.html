The 14-day cooling-off period for subscriptions

Last updated: 18 November 2026

Quick answer

If you sign up for a service online or by phone, you usually have 14 days to change your mind, starting the day after you sign up. If you asked for the service to start straight away, you may have to pay for what you used.

How the cooling-off period works

  • It applies to services you arrange online, by phone or by mail order.
  • It lasts 14 days, starting the day after you enter the contract. Some companies give you longer.
  • If you cancel, the company must refund you within 14 days of being told, using the same payment method unless you agree otherwise.

If the service has already started

If you asked for the service to start during the cooling-off period, the company can keep enough to cover what you’ve used before you cancelled.

Digital content: downloads and streaming

For digital content, you lose the right to cancel once it starts, but only if you agreed to it starting early and acknowledged that you’d lose the right to cancel.

When there’s no cooling-off period

  • you arranged the service in the company’s shop or premises
  • accommodation, vehicle rental, and leisure or catering for a specific date, such as hotel bookings or theatre tickets

How to cancel

Tell the company clearly that you’re cancelling, ideally by email or letter so you have a record. If you phone, note who you spoke to and follow up in writing.

Questions

When does the 14-day cooling-off period start?

For services, it starts the day after you enter the contract.

Do I get all my money back if I cancel?

If the service hasn’t started, yes. If you asked for it to start during the cooling-off period, the company can keep enough to cover what you used.

Can I cancel a download or streaming service in the cooling-off period?

Not once it has started, if you agreed to it starting early and acknowledged that you’d lose the right to cancel.
