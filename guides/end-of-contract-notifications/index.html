End-of-contract notices: why your bill goes up

Last updated: 18 November 2026

Quick answer

Your phone, broadband or pay-TV provider must tell you 10 to 40 days before your contract ends, including what you’ll pay afterwards and its best deals. Use it to haggle or switch before you move onto a higher out-of-contract price.

What the notice tells you

Ofcom requires providers to send an end-of-contract notice, by text, email or letter, between 10 and 40 days before your contract ends. It should include:

  • the date your contract ends
  • what you’ll pay after that date
  • any notice period for leaving
  • the provider’s best deals for you, including prices offered to new customers

Why prices go up at the end of a contract

When a contract ends, you often move onto a standard price that’s higher than your deal. Ofcom’s advice is to check your contract status and shop around for a better deal.

What to do when the notice arrives

  1. Compare the out-of-contract price with the best deals in the notice.
  2. Check prices from other providers.
  3. Ask your provider to match a better offer, or switch. See how to leave a mobile or broadband contract.

Already out of contract?

If your contract has already ended, your provider must still send you its best deals at least once a year.

Questions

When will my provider tell me my contract is ending?

Between 10 and 40 days before it ends, by text, email or letter.

What does an end-of-contract notice include?

The date your contract ends, what you’ll pay afterwards, any notice period for leaving, and the provider’s best deals, including prices for new customers.

I’m already out of contract. Will I hear anything?

Yes. Providers must send out-of-contract customers their best deals at least once a year.
