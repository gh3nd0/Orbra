What to do when a streaming service raises prices

Last updated: [fill in when checked]

Quick answer

Streaming services usually email you before a price rise. Netflix emails a month before the billing date when the new price starts. Before then you can cancel, switch to a cheaper plan, or pause if your service allows it.

How you’ll be told

  • Netflix emails you one month before the billing date on which your price goes up. If you pay through a partner, the partner tells you instead.
  • Spotify emails you in advance with the new price and when it starts.

If you’re not sure the email is real, sign in to your account directly (not through a link) and check your plan and billing details there.

Why the telecoms rules don’t apply

Ofcom’s rule covers phone, broadband and pay-TV providers, not streaming services you pay for directly, like Netflix or Spotify. Most streaming subscriptions are rolling monthly plans that you can cancel at any time, though some also offer annual plans. Each service’s own terms set how it handles price changes.

Your options before the new price starts

  • Cancel before the billing date. See our guides for Netflix, Spotify and Disney+.
  • Switch plan to a cheaper one, such as a plan with ads, if the service has one.
  • Pause if you won’t use it for a while. Disney+ lets monthly subscribers who pay Disney+ directly pause their plan.
  • Rotate: keep one or two services at a time and switch between them.

Questions

How much notice does Netflix give before a price rise?

Netflix emails you one month before the billing date on which your price increases.

Do Ofcom’s price-rise rules cover Netflix or Spotify?

No. Ofcom’s rule covers phone, broadband and pay-TV providers, not streaming services you pay for directly, like Netflix or Spotify.

Can I avoid a streaming price rise?

You can cancel or switch to a cheaper plan before the new price starts. Some services, like Disney+, also let you pause.
