How to avoid being charged after a free trial

Last updated: [fill in when checked]

Quick answer

Cancel before the trial ends, and check the company’s deadline: Apple says at least 24 hours before. Set a reminder the day you sign up. From 1 January 2027, new subscriptions must also send you a reminder before a trial ends.

Before you start a trial

  • Note the date the trial ends and the price you’ll pay after it.
  • Set a reminder for a couple of days before. Orbra can remind you before the first payment.

Cancellation deadlines

  • Apple subscriptions: cancel at least 24 hours before the trial ends. See cancelling on iPhone or iPad.
  • Amazon Prime: ending your free trial stops it turning into a paid membership. See cancelling Amazon Prime.
  • Spotify: cancelling a free trial moves you to the free plan straight away, and you can’t restart that trial.

With some services, cancelling during a trial ends it straight away. If you want to use the whole trial, check the service’s rules first.

New protections from 1 January 2027

For subscriptions taken out on or after 1 January 2027, new rules will require companies to:

  • send you a reminder before a free or discounted trial ends
  • give you a 14-day cooling-off period after a trial turns into a paid subscription
  • let you cancel easily, online if you signed up online

See the new subscription rules for more.

Questions

When should I cancel a free trial?

Before it ends, and check each company’s deadline. Apple says to cancel at least 24 hours before the trial ends.

Will cancelling a trial end it straight away?

Sometimes. For example, cancelling a Spotify free trial moves you to the free plan immediately.

What changes for free trials in 2027?

For subscriptions started on or after 1 January 2027, companies must remind you before a trial ends, and you get 14 days to cancel after it turns into a paid subscription.
