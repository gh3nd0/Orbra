Can you leave a contract after a price rise?

Last updated: [fill in when checked]

Quick answer

If your phone or broadband provider raises your price and your contract didn’t say by how much, or the rise is bigger than it said, you can leave without paying an exit fee. You have 30 days from when the increase takes effect.

When you can leave without a fee

You have the right to leave penalty-free if either of these is true:

  • your contract didn’t say how much the price could go up
  • the rise is bigger than your contract said it would be

If the rise was set out in your contract, for example “£3 a month more from April”, you agreed to it when you signed up, so it doesn’t give you a right to leave.

Notice and deadlines

  • Your provider must tell you about this kind of rise at least 30 days before it happens.
  • From the date the increase takes effect, you have 30 days to decide whether to leave.

How to leave

You can switch to another provider, or take a new deal with your current one, without exit fees. For mobile, the quickest way is a free text: see how to leave a mobile or broadband contract.

On Universal Credit or Pension Credit? Check whether your provider has a social tariff. If you’re eligible, you can move to one without a penalty, even in the middle of a contract.

Questions

Does any price rise let me leave my contract?

No. Only a rise your contract didn’t set out, or one bigger than it set out. A rise written into your contract in pounds and pence doesn’t give you a right to leave.

How long do I have to leave after a price rise?

You have 30 days from the date the increase takes effect.

How much notice must my provider give?

At least 30 days before the increase takes effect.
